// 01. ABOUT

About me

I finished a Cyber Security degree at Nottingham Trent this year and I'm looking for a SOC analyst role. The parts of the job I want are triage, log analysis, and writing the thing up properly afterwards so nobody has to redo the work.

Most of my degree went on building things. For my final year project I set up a Cowrie honeypot dressed up as an IoT device on an isolated network, then attacked it myself from Kali using IoT default-credential wordlists. It logged 552 login attempts. The volume wasn't the interesting bit, since I generated it. What was interesting is that 7 of the 10 usernames and 9 of the 20 passwords I used are hard-coded in the original Mirai source, including xc3511 for XiongMai cameras. It was a contained lab study rather than real internet traffic, and my report says so.

Separately I built a home SOC on Wazuh and Sysmon against a Windows 10 box, ran nine attack scenarios, and wrote an incident report for each one mapped to MITRE ATT&CK. Two of my detections didn't fire when they should have. Both of those are in the repo with a note on what went wrong, because working out why a rule stayed silent taught me more than the seven that worked.

I also co-run HJ Digital Solutions, a small UK IT support business. Every job goes through Jira Service Management and gets a written resolution before it's closed. It's the nearest thing to a real queue I could set up for myself.

I hold SC clearance and BPSS, both from a cleared contract at GXO. I'm working through CompTIA Security+, I'm on Hack The Box most weeks, and my Royal Signals Reserve application has been accepted. I box and train most days, and I bring the same thing to this. I outwork the room.

Security projects

01
Most SOC-relevantCompleted

SOC Detection Lab

A home SOC set up to run the whole analyst workflow. I attacked a Windows 10 box from Kali nine different ways, collected the telemetry with Wazuh and Sysmon, then triaged each alert, mapped it to an ATT&CK technique and wrote it up. Two of my detections didn't work: one host wasn't forwarding its logs, and a custom rule I'd written never fired at all. Both are in the repo with what I found, because that was the useful part.

Output Nine incident reports covering nine ATT&CK techniques, three response playbooks, plus the Wazuh rules I validated and some Sigma drafts.

02

IoT Honeypot & Threat Analysis

A Cowrie honeypot set up to look like a vulnerable IoT device on an isolated VirtualBox network, exposing SSH and Telnet. I ran the dictionary attacks against it myself from Kali, then wrote a Python pipeline to parse the Cowrie JSON into something I could rank and report on. It was a contained lab, not an internet-facing capture, and my report is explicit about that.

Finding 552 attempts, 98.4% of them rejected. 7 of the 10 usernames and 9 of the 20 passwords I used are in the Mirai source dictionary, including xc3511 for XiongMai cameras and vizxv for Dahua DVRs.
